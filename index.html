See every year of your listening in one place.

Drop in the history Spotify gave you. Get your top songs, the song that ruled each month, when you listen, what you keep coming back to, and cards to share.

No export yet? Spotify can take days to send it. See what you will get.

Where do I get the file?

It comes from Spotify, and it is free. You ask for it once.

Open Privacy settings

Sign in at spotify.com, then go to Account and open Privacy settings.

Request the extended history

Under Download your data, tick Extended streaming history and confirm the request by email.

Wait, then drop the zip

Spotify emails you a link. Download the zip and drop it in the box above.

Pick the right option. The shorter Account data download only covers the last year, so it shows far less. You need Extended streaming history. Spotify says it can take up to 30 days to arrive.

Your data stays on your computer

This page has no server to send anything to. It reads the file inside your browser tab.

🔒Never uploaded

The file is read by this page, in your browser. There is no server behind it to receive your history.

🗃️Nothing is stored

No database, no cookies, no analytics. Close the tab and every trace is gone.

🎵Only the music is read

Song, artist, album, play length and time. The IP address, country and device in your file are ignored.

The one exception is album covers. To show them, the page asks Spotify's public cover service for the art of the songs on screen, so the Spotify IDs of those songs (and your IP address, as with any web request) reach Spotify. Your file, your name and your full history never do.

Open your browser's developer tools and watch the Network tab while you load a file: the only requests are the album-cover lookups to Spotify, and your file is never in them. The page's security policy blocks every other network connection, and the code is a handful of plain files with no outside libraries.
